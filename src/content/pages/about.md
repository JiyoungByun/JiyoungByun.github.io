I’m a Ph.D. candidate in Biomedical Engineering at **Johns Hopkins University**, advised by
[**Rama Chellappa**](https://engineering.jhu.edu/faculty/rama-chellappa/). I combine
biomedical expertise with research on language models, vision-language models, and
multi-agent systems to study **how AI capabilities improve and how we can reliably measure
that improvement**. My work spans reasoning, uncertainty calibration, and inference-time
computation. I also examine the assumptions behind evaluation, particularly when reference
answers are unavailable and the evaluators themselves may be unreliable.

I bring these questions into clinical and scientific settings through hands-on system
development and interdisciplinary collaboration. With Microsoft collaborators, I developed
hallucination-aware calibration methods for medical vision-language models. At Merck, I built
scientific agents and investigated how self-critique and evaluator choices affect conclusions
about their outputs. With ophthalmologists at the Wilmer Eye Institute and Aravind Eye
Hospital, I worked on smartphone-based cataract screening evaluated prospectively in rural
India. Across these projects, I **translate domain-specific challenges into testable machine
learning questions**, connecting method development, controlled experiments, and evaluation
under practical constraints.

I’m seeking **Research Scientist** roles where I can advance the capabilities and reliability
of foundation models and agentic systems for health and science.
